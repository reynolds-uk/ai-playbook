# Leader check, 28 September 2026

*A worked example for a fictional business, written by the plugin in a test run with a simulated user. The people and figures are invented for the test.*

**The business:** PE- or investor-backed, with most of its value in people's expertise and time. Check run with Sarah Collins.

## Where you stand

**Where you point it: gap.** AI goes where vendors suggest or where someone is keen, not where the work costs most. Tool: the four questions.

**What it earns: gap.** AI shows up in the plan only as cheaper hours, so any saving is likely to end up in lower fees rather than in your margin. Tool: where the revenue is.

**What reaches the accounts: gap.** You have estimates of hours saved, but finance can't yet point to them in the accounts. Tool: the two columns.

**What good looks like: gap.** Your evidence that the AI's work is right is mostly the vendor's word. Tool: the test set.

**Who's on the hook: gap.** Nobody is named for what AI does. IT looks after it, but nobody is answerable for its results. Tool: the register.

**Where judgement comes from: gap.** Nobody has yet thought about where your next experienced people will learn their judgement. In an expertise business, that is the thing you sell. Tool: the curriculum exercise.

**What you own: gap.** What sets you apart is your people and client relationships, but none of it is written down in a form the AI runs on. Tool: the layer you own.

**How you decide to scale: gap.** A pilot that looks good gets rolled out, with no criteria set in advance and nothing written down that would make you stop. Tool: stages and gates.

## The autonomy map: Assuming

The AP-invoice agent posts supplier invoices under £2,000 with no one checking. But there is no test set of your own and no named person accountable for it, so its autonomy rests on trust, not evidence.

## Where to start

Build a test set for the AP-invoice agent: thirty to fifty real invoices, with the right outcome marked by your best people in finance. Then put one name against every AI tool, starting with that agent. That person should be able to pull the agent back if its results slip. Until both are done, hold the agent's autonomy where it is and give it no more. Once that's in place, the next job for an expertise business is the curriculum exercise: decide which work you keep for people because it teaches.

## Three questions for the team

1. How do we know the AI's work is right, and whose cases prove it?
2. For each thing AI does in our name, who is the one person accountable?
3. Which piece of work, if it took half the time, would our customers actually notice, and what happens five minutes before and after it?

## A sentence for the board

"On the autonomy map, we're in Assuming. We have no strong ground yet; our biggest gap is what good looks like. This is where I think we are; tell me if you disagree."
